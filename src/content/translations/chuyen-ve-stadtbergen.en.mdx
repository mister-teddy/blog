## Why Stadtbergen?

To my (almost ex) boyfriend, going from Munich to Augsburg to Stadtbergen felt like a downgrade. But my dream is to live in a house at the edge of a forest (which is also why I chose Bavaria, in Germany). Stadtbergen is a small, lush green town right against Augsburg, and moving here I (comforting myself) decided this was an upgrade.

## Finding a flat in Germany is as hard as finding a job

I'm exaggerating. Finding a job in Germany is harder 🙃

But for a foreigner who doesn't speak German, it really is an ordeal, because supply is lower than demand, and when there are plenty of options landlords tend to prefer people they think will stay long and have solid finances (which foreigners with no family in Germany usually don't).

A few highlights before I settled on Stadtbergen:
- **Shaken down for a €529 cash service fee at the Hotelturm.** It's a very famous building, but if you don't know it: 35 floors, 115 m tall, and it stands out because houses in Bavaria are usually 6 floors at most. It was rushed up for the 1972 Munich Olympics, and half the tower is rented out as the Dorint hotel (which is why people also call it the Dorint tower). I liked it for the panorama, but I didn't like how unprofessional the letting side was, so I skipped it (actually I got skipped, but fine, let's all skip it).
  <ProseImage src="/blog/chuyen-ve-stadtbergen/hotelturm.jpg" alt="The Hotelturm rising above red roofs and green hills in Augsburg" />
  *(One look and you can tell which one is the Hotelturm, right)*
- Almost signed a tiny 24 m² studio for nearly €1,000. For that price in the suburbs you can rent a multi-room flat, with its own kitchen, three times the size. But I really liked how enthusiastically the salesperson helped (probably because it's expensive per m² 🫠).
  <ProseImage src="/blog/chuyen-ve-stadtbergen/can-24m2.jpg" alt="Floor plan of Wohnung 60: one 24.76 m² room, living/kitchen/sleeping 14.50 m², €940 warm rent" />
- While I was about to become homeless, a colleague of mine had just viewed this flat in Stadtbergen and was about to rent it, but the landlord was busy and replied late, and my colleague signed somewhere else. Then they introduced me to the landlord, and I closed on it so fast that the landlord asked: *"You viewed it three days ago. You didn't think you'd be holding the keys today, right?"* The landlord is also in IT, and incredibly sweet 😊 But there's more I haven't told you 🙂: the photos on the Kleinanzeigen listing were a bit of a fake-out. ## From a construction site to an IKEA cardboard depot

Yes. I chose software, so I'm afraid of anything to do with hardware. Only after signing the contract did I realise this was an empty flat, and I would have to sort out the bed, the wardrobe, the table, all the way to the curtains and the washing machine...

### The washing machine ordeal

<video src="/blog/chuyen-ve-stadtbergen/may-giat.mp4" controls playsinline preload="metadata" width="360"></video>

If you didn't know: when you buy a new washing machine they bolt four transit-bolt things onto the back for shipping, and if you don't take them off and just use it, it turns into a tractor. It took a few days before I could wash a basin of dirty clothes, and those days I slept badly, convinced I'd bought a dud and would somehow have to haul it back and have a new one shipped 🥲 While I cut my hand scooping soap out of a glass jar a friend had lent me, because supermarkets are closed on Sunday, I missed my days in Vietnam and the technicians from Điện máy xanh 🥺

### At IKEA, they have everything

The saving grace is that IKEA delivers all the way to your bedroom, and they have everything (I even bought the washing machine at IKEA). And as it happened, the week I had no bed, the company had an event in Berlin, so I didn't have to sleep on the floor for too long 😆 After many weekends + sweat + tears + blood, the room quickly went from a construction site to an IKEA wrapping-paper "depot":

Since I'm already off topic, I'll go all the way 😆: lazy about my own housework, diligent when it's for the elders. Wardrobe not assembled, homework not done, but I chose to go to the circus with Klaus ([my first landlord in Germany](/blog/chancenkarte/#finding-housing-in-germany)). While helping Klaus tidy that gorgeous garden, I left my phone outside and it got water in it (not the first time). At the circus I felt like I was sitting on a fire (no phone, no internet, no AI, no cash on me (yes, my only debit card lives inside the phone), no map, and I couldn't take the train because I had no Deutschlandticket), and then the next morning I woke up and the phone worked normally again. And from then on I officially became a OnePlus fan 🫶

## The #1 person I always trust: Claude Code

I often complain to friends that ever since AI can code, I've lost the motivation to work, but honestly, without Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini I don't know how I'd live 🫠 Claude handles everything for me, from ~~food and clothes~~ a mountain of paperwork (I don't think I've mentioned that I had to sign up for electricity and wifi myself (and speaking of wifi, in Germany it takes more than two weeks from signup before you can actually use it 🫠)), choosing furniture, fixing the washing machine (yes), finding a flatmate (yes, I moved out of a WG because I wanted to live alone, but the rent was too high so I turned the new flat into a WG 🫠), drafting the lease, watering the plants properly, using the wood stove without CO poisoning or burning down the landlord's house...
