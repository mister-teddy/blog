## Why Germany

When I got my [German visa](/blog/chancenkarte/) and shared my journey with everyone, the top 5 questions I got were:

<ol reversed>
  <li>don't remember</li>
  <li>don't remember</li>
  <li>"Going to study abroad?"</li>
  <li>"Why Germany?"</li>
  <li>"Do you have relatives there?"</li>
</ol>

It seems most people really care about whether you have relatives in some strange faraway place, but I don't. And I think #2 is a really good question, and I just love having to answer it over and over again 🤣

- Why Germany, and not the US, UK, or Australia? Because (as far as I know) Germany is the only developed country with a work visa that doesn't require you to already have an employment contract. The US is the promised land for programmers, yes, but I don't recall any visa that lets you enter first and then look for a job.

- So why abroad, and not in Vietnam? Sometimes I wonder whether anyone thinks I'm a foreigner-worshipper, but I just happen to love watching foreign films, learning foreign languages, and traveling abroad. As for food, I still prefer Vietnamese food (yes, my mom's cooking 🤰🏻❤️), and my patriotism is still strong 🇻🇳 I'm still young, and I have no plans to start a family, so I figure that before I grow old and die and leave nothing behind, I should at least experience enough — and you can't experience enough if you only stay in your own country, nor on a domestic engineer's salary.

And why not study abroad? Honestly, that was once an option 😂 But while waiting on the APS procedure, I discovered the Opportunity Card track (a sign from heaven??) and I thought it was a better fit, because if I went to study abroad now, the final target would still be to work as an engineer in Germany — just costing more money and time. Thanks to this track, by the time I got the email with my APS appointment (December), I had already settled into my job in Germany (November).

> Speaking of the weather (a bit off-topic but it's fun): I thought the 16–20°C I landed in was the temperature of this cold country (the way 25–35°C is Vietnam's temperature). A few months later, when the temperature got close to 0, I finally understood that the range here is wider — winter can hit -10°C and summer can reach 35°C (yes, Geography was never my favorite subject). That's actually one of the things I quite like here, because you can experience every kind of weather without going anywhere, from below-zero blanketed in white snow to 30°C in the middle of a summer night. As a result, the clothes you see on the street are more fun too, from head-to-toe high-collar coats like in the fantasy-magic films I love watching, to shorts and tank tops. And it seems people here aren't afraid to dress differently. I remember when I was young (yes, I'm at the stage where I can say "when I was young" but still don't feel grown up enough), I used to wear shirts with stylized dragon-and-snake patterns, and the moment I stepped outside all eyes were on me 👀 And the climate here is very dry; I'd never had to put anything on my face until my first day out, when I realized my facial skin was peeling off in flakes like fish scales, and I had to rush straight into the supermarket to find a tube of <ProseImage src="/blog/sau-6-thang-o-duc/nivea-men-gel.png" alt="A tube of NIVEA MEN Sensitive Face & Beard Moisture Gel" width={24} inline /> And another impression of mine about Germany is that food here is crazy expensive but cosmetics are dirt cheap — worth lugging a few extra kilos of luggage to bring soap over from Vietnam.

> The first time I shared that aerial Google Maps photo of a house in Germany with a former colleague, I got the reaction "smells like a fish pond" 😔 But am I **criticizing**? When I share these thoughts I think I'm **praising** Germany, and **hoping** that one day my own country will develop like this too.

> Just kidding — this month wasn't free at all: sitting around sending out CVs, prepping for interviews, doing Upwork on the side — super stressful, no joke!!!

## Month 2, thrilled to have a job

After 5 interview rounds,

> The first 3 rounds were mostly just talking and I think I only did well enough to scrape into round 4. Round 4 was a take-home assignment; I tried to show off everything I had, and after 2 days of coding day and night (no vibe coding, okay 😎), my (future, at the time) boss was really impressed with [my submission](https://mister-teddy.github.io/reonic-ev-charging-simulation/). The day I interviewed for round 5 was also the day I got the job offer.

Thursday morning, I caught the train from Munich Hbf to Augsburg Hbf for the final interview round. This was lunch before my interview:

![A schnitzel served with potato salad, lingonberry jam, a lemon wedge, and a cup of coffee with milk](/blog/sau-6-thang-o-duc/do-an-1.jpg)

Like the talking rounds, I didn't think I did all that well. Before leaving I asked when I'd hear back, and the boss said about 1–2 weeks. What could be more exciting??? 🤩

## Month 3, cold

My first proper Christmas. Actually this was somewhere around the end of month 2, but since the section above was getting too long I pushed it over here to even out the content :))

By this point I'd moved to Augsburg, and went back to Sauerlach to celebrate Christmas with my old landlord:

![A vintage-style 'Grüße aus Sauerlach' postcard: a cozy dining table, snow-covered Bavarian wooden houses, and a snowy white village](/blog/sau-6-thang-o-duc/sauerlach.png)

There were a lot of public holidays around this time (it seems most German holidays are crammed into this period): Christmas and New Year, and people still had leftover vacation days so they took plenty of time off — the office was deserted.

> Germany really is paradise for full-time employees, because if I still hadn't gotten a job offer at this point I'd also be "on holiday" but without pay 🙏. In Vietnam, on the other hand, running your own business or freelancing is the way to go; people tend to rank employees a tier lower (personal opinion, from a few rounds of administrative procedures).

I remember back in school (bad at Geography), I always thought October–December was winter and January was spring. But no — here, January–February is the peak of winter. The snow falls pure white, very beautiful and strange because in Vietnam you'd never in your life see snow on a rooftop, and it's incredibly cold. Stepping outside, you either keep your hands in your pockets or wear gloves, otherwise your hand might as well get sawed off.

Another fun thing is that the gap between day and night in Germany is huge too — 4 p.m. in winter is as dark as 8 p.m. in Vietnam, so during this time I'd often feel exhausted after only 5 hours of work because I kept thinking I was doing overtime 🫠 (and now it's midsummer and it's 8 p.m. and I'm doing overtime without even realizing it 🫠).

Even though I don't like the cold, I feel comfortable when: it's cold outside, the windows are shut, the heater is on, I'm under a blanket and asleep. More so than when: it's hot outside, the windows are shut, the AC is on, under a blanket asleep. Because the AC is noisy (probably because mine is a cheap one), while the heater isn't (or at least it's white noise), and if I turn the AC off then in Vietnam's humid climate I sweat a lot in my sleep => can't sleep at all.

## Month 456, sad

I'm at the stage where seeing German text — even though I don't understand any of it — feels really familiar 🤷‍♂️

Houses, scenery, public transport, clothes, Western people... aren't all that different to me anymore. I've also lost the habit of converting EUR back to VND (this bottle of water costs 90k, this bowl of phở costs 600k...) and I think that's a bad habit because I've started spending more 💸

AI is so damn good at coding now that I write prose more than I write code these days (I chose to be a programmer because I prefer writing code over writing prose 😭).

During the week I'm really busy but on weekends I don't know what to do, so in my free time I often hunt for plane tickets (sometimes they're as cheap as a bowl of phở 🤷‍♂️, and combined with a hostel combo you can put together a whole Europe travel album without spending a fortune):
